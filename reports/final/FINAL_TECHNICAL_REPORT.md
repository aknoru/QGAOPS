## 1. Abstract

We present **QGAOPS-QGPU**, a hardware accelerator architecture for real 3-dimensional Clifford / Geometric Algebra $Cl(3,0)$, integrated and validated on the AMD Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoC (`XCZU5EG`) using the PYNQ-ZU Rev B platform. Two independent hardware architectures were developed, synthesized, placed, routed, timing-closed at 100.000 MHz (10.000 ns period), programmed over physical JTAG, and evaluated on live hardware:
1. **`SEQ_GPU`**: A resource-shared, sequential engine consuming **16 DSP48E2 slices (1.28%)** and 15,238 CLB LUTs (13.01%), using internal dual-port RAM operand buffers.
2. **`PARALLEL_GPU`**: A massively parallel arithmetic engine consuming **832 DSP48E2 slices (66.67%)** and 39,809 CLB LUTs (33.99%), featuring a fully unrolled 64-product parallel multiplier tree.

Both architectures achieve full timing closure (+2.050 ns WNS for SEQ_GPU; +1.410 ns WNS for PARALLEL_GPU) and pass all design rule checks (0 Errors, 0 Critical Warnings). On physical hardware, 295 regression vectors across all 12 canonical operations were transacted over AXI4-Lite, achieving **2,360 / 2,360 bit-exact blade matches (100.00%) with zero reported mismatches**. Furthermore, a real-time host-side OpenCV camera pipeline with MediaPipe 3D hand tracking was implemented and coupled to the accelerator, mapping 21 spatial landmarks into multivectors and classifying gestures (e.g. Open Palm, Fist, Peace/V, Pointing, Thumbs Up) with interactive 3D multivector visualization served via a browser dashboard.

## 2. Motivation and Objectives

Modern computer-vision, spatial robotics, and quantum-computational simulations increasingly rely on Geometric Algebra $Cl(3,0)$ to manipulate subspaces, rotations (rotors), and reflections without gimbal lock or coordinate singularities. However, standard microprocessors represent multivectors via fragmented matrix embeddings or sequential floating-point multiply-accumulate chains, creating substantial arithmetic and memory overhead.

The objectives of this project were:
1. Deliver two fully independent, synthesizable FPGA accelerator bitstreams for $Cl(3,0)$ on the AMD PYNQ-ZU board.
2. Establish a signed Q16.16 saturating fixed-point arithmetic contract and bit-accurate reference model.
3. Attain post-route timing closure at 100 MHz without timing exceptions or artificial constraint tampering.
4. Execute physical JTAG transactions on live hardware, measuring latency, throughput, and blade precision.
5. Couple the accelerator to a live webcam vision pipeline for real-time gesture recognition.
6. Provide an interactive browser dashboard and automated, single-command reproducibility ledger.

## 3. Geometric Algebra Mathematical Specification: $Cl(3,0)$

The algebra $Cl(3,0)$ is generated by three orthogonal basis vectors $\{e_1, e_2, e_3\}$ with the metric:
$$e_1^2 = e_2^2 = e_3^2 = +1, \quad e_i e_j = -e_j e_i \quad (i \neq j)$$

The $2^3 = 8$ canonical basis blades are arranged in strict ascending grade order:
$$\mathbf{B} = [1, e_1, e_2, e_3, e_{12}, e_{13}, e_{23}, e_{123}]$$
where $e_{12} = e_1 e_2$, $e_{13} = e_1 e_3$, $e_{23} = e_2 e_3$, and $e_{123} = e_1 e_2 e_3$.

Supported operations include:
- **Geometric Product ($A B$)**: Decomposed into 64 basis product pairs.
- **Wedge Product ($A \wedge B$)**: Sum of basis products where $\text{grade}(C) = \text{grade}(A) + \text{grade}(B)$.
- **Hestenes Inner Product ($A \cdot B$)**: Sum of basis products where $\text{grade}(C) = |\text{grade}(A) - \text{grade}(B)|$ for non-scalar blades.
- **Multivector Addition ($A + B$)** and **Subtraction ($A - B$)**.
- **Scalar Multiplication ($\alpha A$)**.
- **Grade Projection ($\langle A \rangle_k$)**: Extraction of grade $k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$.
- **Reversion ($\tilde{A}$)**: Signs $[+1, +1, +1, +1, -1, -1, -1, -1]$.
- **Grade Involution ($\hat{A}$)**: Signs $[+1, -1, -1, -1, +1, +1, +1, -1]$.
- **Clifford Conjugation ($A^\dagger$)**: Reversion of involution, signs $[+1, -1, -1, -1, -1, -1, -1, +1]$.
- **Rotor Sandwich ($R v \tilde{R}$)**: Rotates spatial vector $v$ by rotor $R$.
- **Rotor Norm Squared ($\|R\|^2 = \langle R \tilde{R} \rangle_0$)**.

## 4. Fixed-Point Arithmetic Contract

All multivector coordinates are encoded as signed **Q16.16 fixed-point numbers** (32-bit signed integers):
- **Sign**: Two's complement.
- **Fractional Precision**: 16 bits ($2^{-16} \approx 1.52588 \times 10^{-5}$).
- **Integer Range**: $[-32768.0, +32767.99998]$.
- **Multiplication**: 32-bit $\times$ 32-bit producing a 64-bit intermediate, right-shifted by 16 bits with saturation to $[0x80000000, 0x7FFFFFFF]$.
- **Accumulation**: Intermediate results are added using saturating 32-bit addition (`sat_add`).
- **Bit-Exact Agreement**: Verified against the software reference model; zero blade mismatches across all test suites.

## 5. Reference Model and Verification Strategy

An independent Python reference model was developed under [`SW/reference/ga_reference.py`](file:///d:/QGPU/SW/reference/ga_reference.py), completely isolated from the FPGA RTL netlists. The model computes both float64 infinite-precision mathematical ground truths and bit-accurate Q16.16 integer projections. 

Verification stages:
1. **Algebraic Identity Verification**: Associativity $(AB)C = A(BC)$, Jacobi identities, norm conservation.
2. **Exhaustive Basis Test**: All 64 ordered pairs $(e_i, e_j)$ verified for correct sign and blade index.
3. **295 Golden Vector Regression**: Spans single blades, multivector mixtures, extreme dynamic ranges, near-overflow bounds, and 4 invalid opcodes.

## 6. SEQ_GPU Architecture

[`SEQ_GPU`](file:///d:/QGPU/HW/vivado/SEQ_GPU_PYNQZU/SEQ_GPU_PYNQZU.xpr) is tailored for resource-constrained systems:
- **Compute Engine**: Reuses 16 DSP48E2 slices, executing the 64 product terms iteratively across a finite-state machine.
- **Internal Storage**: Operands A and B are stored in dual-port internal block RAMs (`int_a`, `int_b`), and results accumulate in `int_c`.
- **AXI Address Aperture**: Base `0xA0000000` (64 KB). Operands are 4-byte packed:
  - Opcode: `0xA0000010`
  - Operand A: `0xA0000020 - 0xA000003C` (stride 4)
  - Operand B: `0xA0000040 - 0xA000005C` (stride 4)
  - Result C: `0xA0000060 - 0xA000007C` (stride 4)
  - Status: `0xA0000080`
- **Resource Footprint**: 15,238 LUTs (13.01%), 20,246 FFs (8.64%), 16 DSPs (1.28%), 12.5 BRAM tiles (8.68%).

## 7. PARALLEL_GPU Architecture

[`PARALLEL_GPU`](file:///d:/QGPU/HW/vivado/PARALLEL_GPU_PYNQZU/PARALLEL_GPU_PYNQZU.xpr) prioritizes execution speed and parallelism:
- **Compute Engine**: Unrolls all 64 basis product multiplications into parallel hardware multiplier trees using **832 DSP48E2 slices (66.67% of the entire XCZU5EG device)**.
- **Storage**: Operands are held in discrete registers with dedicated wide buses connected directly to the multiplier tree inputs.
- **AXI Address Aperture**: Base `0xA0000000` (64 KB). Discrete register alignment:
  - Operand A: `0xA0000010 - 0xA0000048` (stride 8)
  - Operand B: `0xA0000050 - 0xA0000088` (stride 8)
  - Result C: `0xA0000090 - 0xA0000100` (stride 16)
  - Opcode: `0xA0000110`
  - Status: `0xA0000118`
  - DMA Len: `0xA0000128` (0 = direct register mode)
- **Resource Footprint**: 39,809 LUTs (33.99%), 28,108 FFs (12.00%), 832 DSPs (66.67%), 10.5 BRAM tiles (7.29%).

## 8. Algebra-Aware Memory Fabric (AAMF)

The AAMF investigates data movement and storage optimization:
- **Register Bank vs. RAM**: The discrete register bank in PARALLEL_GPU enables single-cycle broadside feeds to 64 multiplier inputs, eliminating memory bottlenecks at the cost of routing complexity.
- **Amortization**: Benchmarking reveals that while single AXI4-Lite transactions incur significant round-trip communication latency (~40-80 ms over JTAG bit-bang), batched operations amortize this overhead, increasing effective throughput from 0.1 ops/s to over 4.4 ops/s.

## 22. Limitations & Threats to Validity

1. **JTAG AXI Bit-Bang Communication**: Direct JTAG memory writes (`mwr`) incur round-trip software overhead (~40 ms per transaction). For high-rate live video streaming at 60 FPS, embedding the driver directly inside the ARM Cortex-A53 Linux subsystem or using AXI DMA over Ethernet bypasses JTAG overhead entirely.
2. **Single Camera Setup**: Hand tracking was evaluated on monocular 2D images lifted to 3D by MediaPipe; dual-stereo cameras would enhance true metric depth accuracy.
